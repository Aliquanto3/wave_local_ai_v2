# Spike: Which two search tools are obtainable, archivable, and what does each send upstream?

## Question

Which two web-search tools, one self-hosted and one hosted API, can this project use on free or low-cost terms; does each return a response that can be archived with its row in a form from which the score recomputes offline (Methodology 17); and what does each actually forward to third parties when queried, so the self-hosted option's egress is checked rather than assumed?

## Decision

Which two tools the web-research suite's adapters implement, or whether the tool comparison is published `out-of-scope-this-release` because fewer than two are obtainable (the epic's second conditional trigger); what each row records as its egress destination; and whether either tool's terms forbid archiving or republishing its responses in the CC-BY 4.0 reference bundle.

## Bounds

- Evidence needed: for each candidate (a self-hosted metasearch instance first, then hosted APIs with a free or low-cost tier), its current pricing and quota page at a dated retrieval, its terms on storing and redistributing responses, one live response captured and saved to show what an archived response contains (result URLs, snippets, and whether page content must be fetched separately to judge against), its rate-limit and error responses as documented, and, for the self-hosted option, which upstream engines it queries by default and what it sends them.
- Stop when: two tools are shown to meet the cost, archivability and terms constraints with their egress stated, or every candidate examined is shown to fail one of them and the shortfall is stated.

### Evidence

- Of the self-hosted candidates, only SearXNG is a real option. It holds no index: a "self-hosted" query leaves the machine to every enabled engine, from the operator's public IP, with no key. At the pinned commit the default destinations are duckduckgo.com, search.brave.com, cse.google.com (and www.google.com for the CSE token) through a blackle.com partner id, the locale's wikipedia.org host, and query.wikidata.org.
- SearXNG reaches its default engines by scraping their websites, not through any API. Its JSON output (`/search?q=...&format=json&language=fr`) holds per result `url`, `title`, `content` (snippet) and the engines that returned it. It has no page text. Results are not deterministic: each engine has a 3 s timeout, and which engines answer in time changes the merged list. Archiving makes this irrelevant to recompute but not to a live re-run.
- Each hosted API was checked against three tests: the terms allow it, it is obtainable, and it is low cost. Brave fails on terms (no storage beyond transient use, no redistribution, no benchmarking AI). Exa fails on terms (no copying or publishing anything obtained). SerpApi most likely fails on terms (no reproducing or copying any portion of the Service). Google CSE fails on obtainability (closed to new customers, ends 2027-01-01). Bing fails on obtainability (retired 2025-08-11). DuckDuckGo fails on obtainability (no results API). Perplexity Search has no free tier, and its terms were unreadable (403). Kagi's terms and price are unverified, and it is the costliest.
- Four hosted APIs pass the terms test for keeping an archive: Mojeek (explicit storage rights on its Business plan, own index), Tavily (terms silent on Output; free tier), Serper (terms silent; free credits, but scraped Google) and Jina (no restriction on Output, but its backends are scraped SERPs).
- Of those four, Mojeek alone sends the query to one destination that does not log IPs (per a site-wide privacy policy whose API scope is unstated) and grants storage in writing. Tavily keeps the query and may train on it. Serper and Jina pass the query on to Google or other SERP backends.
- No provider's terms grant a right to republish results under CC-BY 4.0, and none could. A response's titles and snippets are third-party text: Mojeek and Jina both say publishers' rights are unaffected. So the CC-BY 4.0 bundle can carry search responses verbatim only on a legal basis the project does not have from any provider.
- Mojeek, SearXNG and Serper return snippets only. Judging "against the retrieved sources" over full page text needs a separate page fetch per result URL. That fetch is a further egress to each result's host, and it adds more third-party text to the archive. Tavily, Exa and Jina return page content inline.

### Decisions

- Web research is retrieve-then-answer (owner Q34 (a), `aidd_docs/tasks/2026_10/2026_10_01_autonomous-slicing/owner-questions.md`): the harness issues the search and archives the response, so a response is a function of the query alone. Only the search endpoint's terms and egress matter; no tool-calling surface is in scope.
- The tools are one self-hosted and one hosted API (epic Boundaries; story order 10 acceptance). Fewer than two obtainable tools publishes the comparison `out-of-scope-this-release` (epic conditional trigger).
- Suite items and the reference bundle carry CC-BY 4.0 (PRD acceptance; owner answer in `owner-questions.md`, "Bundle, licences and citation"). The PRD already states a "no redistribution" outcome for benchmark subsets: "publishes item ids, content hashes and scores without the item text".
- Every search query is the repo's own, never client material (epic Excludes). Query egress therefore exposes no client data.

### Assumptions

- SearXNG sends a browser-like generated User-Agent to engines. This is inferred from the `User-Agent` header the DDG module reads; the generator was not read.
- "Full storage rights" on Mojeek's Business plan covers keeping the archive indefinitely for recompute. It is not read as covering public redistribution, which no Mojeek text found addresses.
- Mojeek's no-IP logging statement applies to API requests too. The policy does not say.
- A free Tavily account, a free Serper account and Mojeek pay-as-you-go are available to a sole individual without a sales contact. Only the pages were read; no account was opened.

## Outcome

- Result: two tools pass by desk evidence: **SearXNG (self-hosted) and Mojeek Web Search API (hosted)**, so the tool comparison is not triggered out of scope. SearXNG costs nothing, and its JSON output can be archived once enabled. Its egress is not local: each query goes, from the instance's public IP, to DuckDuckGo, Brave's website, Google CSE (through a third party's partner id), Wikipedia and Wikidata, by scraping. That egress must be recorded per row as the engine list. Mojeek costs £2 to £3 per 1,000 queries pay-as-you-go. It runs its own index (one egress destination), grants storage in writing on its Business plan only, takes EN/FR/DE through `lb` and `rb`, and documents a JSON error status for quota exhaustion. If Mojeek is refused, the fallback hosted tool is Tavily (free, terms silent on storage, keeps and may train on queries). Brave, Exa, SerpApi, Google CSE, Bing and DuckDuckGo each fail one constraint, as quoted above. No tool's terms permit republishing its responses' text in the CC-BY 4.0 bundle. The archive can be kept and recomputed from, but publishing it verbatim is not covered by any provider's terms or by the snippets' authors.
- Confidence: medium. Terms and pricing come from the providers' own pages, read 2026-10-02, and SearXNG's egress comes from source at a pinned commit. But the Bounds require one live response per tool, documented rate-limit behaviour and observed egress; none was captured (no account, no install, no call allowed). Mojeek's 429 or rate-limit response shape is not documented beyond "ERROR: Daily Limit Reached". Mojeek's terms on republication were not found.
- Remaining uncertainty: (1) a live SearXNG response and its observed upstream hosts under the chosen engine set; (2) a live Mojeek response, its rate-limit or error shape, and whether a sole individual can buy the Business plan; (3) whether Mojeek grants republication of titles and snippets (needs a written answer from Mojeek); (4) the owner choices below.
