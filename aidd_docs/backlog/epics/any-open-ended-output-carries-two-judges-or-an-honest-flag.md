# Epic: Any open-ended output carries two judges, or an honest flag

Given any open-ended model output, a consultant gets a judged score from two cloud judges of different model families with their agreement recorded — or from the single judge independence allows, visibly flagged as such — and every judge call behind that score is reproducible from the row alone.

## Context and Value

The audience is the one the PRD's user story names directly: "As a consultant, I want open-ended task scores backed by two independent LLM judges with agreement reported, so that a client's engineer can't dismiss a score as one model's opinion." The product brief makes it one of the three legs of the bet — "judged scores always carry inter-judge agreement" (`aidd_docs/product/wave-local-ai-v2.md`, Product Bet).

The machinery under that leg is now largely built. What this refinement changes is who does the judging, and why the last step has never run.

Verified current state, at `0f849c8`:

- **The judge protocol, independence and agreement machinery are shipped and provider-agnostic.** `judge.py` takes an injected `JudgeBackend` and names no provider; `judge_protocol.py` holds the versioned prompt templates and rubrics; `agreement.py` holds the statistic and the contested threshold; `row_contract.JUDGED_FIELDS` makes the judge block conditional-but-complete, so a row that declares itself judged owes every field or is refused. `judge.select_judges` refuses a judge of the subject's own family by name. Orders 1 to 5 of this epic are `done`.
- **The provider bindings sit behind exactly one seam.** `judge_backends.py` states in its own docstring that it is the only judge-path module importing a client, "so adding a third judge provider, or swapping one out, reopens this file and no other". That seam is what makes this re-targeting a bounded change rather than a rewrite.
- **The two providers wired are Mistral and Google, and Methodology 11 has just disqualified both as judges.** `roster.KNOWN_FAMILIES` is `frozenset({"qwen", "mistral", "google"})` and `MODEL_FAMILIES` keys three dated ids; `cost.PRICE_TABLES` prices `mistral` and `google`; `settings.py` reads `MISTRAL_API_KEY` and `GOOGLE_API_KEY`. Every one of those is correct for a subject and wrong for a judge.
- **The judged probe has never run.** `judge_probe.py` is written, but `aidd_docs/results/` holds no `judge-probe-reference.jsonl`. Order 6 is `ready` and has been unable to produce a row: its judges are the pair the PRD has now retired.

The re-targeting itself comes from the amended Methodology 11 and from gap 9 of `context_input/publication_gap_brief.md`. The roster epic `every-size-class-spans-two-families-or-says-it-does-not` puts `gemma`, `granite`, `ministral`, `lfm2` and `phi` on the local roster beside `qwen`, because a publication needs two families per size class. That composition rule is what disqualifies the old pair: Google cannot judge a Gemma row, and Ministral makes *mistral* a local subject family — which is not a temporary awkwardness but the permanent reason Mistral can never judge again. The pair is therefore **GLM via Z.ai's direct API and DeepSeek via DeepSeek's own**, chosen because both families are independent of every model the target roster holds, local or cloud. A small paid budget, on the order of ten dollars per campaign at catalogue rates, is confirmed (PRD Dependencies), so the free-tier-only constraint is lifted while rate-limit survival is not: paid tiers are rate-limited too.

Google and Mistral become **cloud subjects only, and never judges again**. Nothing built for them is wasted by that. The Google spike (`google-ai-studio-api-surface-is-confirmed-live`, `done`) answered the catalog, the dated id, the sampling controls and the finish-reason mapping for a provider this project still calls on every run; `google_client.py`, its price table and its `provider` value keep their full value as subject infrastructure, and `a-second-cloud-provider-answers-suite-items-as-a-subject` remains the story that put a second cloud subject on the comparison. What changes is that neither client is ever bound as a judge backend.

Three neighbours have already written this epic's seam into their own boundaries. The row epic excludes criteria 10 and 11 explicitly and leaves the row schema open for the fields they add. The pitch epic's quality view waits on judge model ids, agreement and the single-judge flag, and commits to refusing to render a judged score that carries neither. And `judge-scoring-with-inter-judge-agreement-proves-judged-machinery`, story 2 of the suite epic, is `ready` and still cannot start: it asks for two independent judges over the rewriting suite, and until this epic's pair answers a live call, neither exists in a form the roster permits.

The value has two parts. The first is reach: deterministic scoring covers classification and translation, and every remaining use case the PRD lists — rewriting, document comparison, code generation, agentic planning, agentic tool calling, web research, RAG answer generation — is open-ended and therefore unscorable until judges the roster permits exist. The second is the defensibility itself. A single judge's score is one model's opinion, and a judge scoring output from its own family is the precise objection a client's engineer raises first. Independence and agreement are not quality improvements to a judged score; they are what makes it admissible.

## Boundaries

- Includes: **the judge pair, Z.ai's GLM and DeepSeek, each called through that provider's own direct API**, under the same pinning discipline criterion 12 already imposes — a dated model id read from the provider's live catalog rather than from its documentation, a pre-flight that refuses a run when that id is absent, sampling pinned by the caller, one typed error with an unavailable-model subclass, and a dated list-price entry keyed by the literal dated id. Two clients, mirroring the shape `mistral_client.py` and `google_client.py` already share, bound at the `judge_backends.py` seam and nowhere else.
- Includes: **the two spikes those clients need, before either is written.** Z.ai's and DeepSeek's API surfaces are each confirmed against live calls rather than documentation, mirroring the completed Google spike's question list: live catalog endpoint and auth, dated non-floating ids, seed or determinism controls and what is applied when omitted, rate limits and their retry hints on the paid tier, finish-reason mapping including any block or safety reason, and catalogue pricing. Plus one question the Google spike did not have to ask as sharply — **data-use and retention terms**, because the PRD's egress non-goal depends on the answer: only the repo's own suite items ever leave the machine, and a provider that trains on or retains submitted text changes what that sentence means.
- Includes: **the retirement of Mistral and Google as judges.** Neither client is ever bound as a judge backend again, in this release or by configuration. The clients, their price tables, their `provider` values and the spike that produced them stay, as cloud-subject infrastructure; only the judge bindings go.
- Includes: **per-judge-call row constraints**, additive to the judge block already shipped. Every judge call records the provider that actually answered — no fallback routing, and where any router is ever used its provider order is fixed, fallbacks disabled, and the answering provider written onto the row; the reasoning effort the call was issued with, disabled or minimal; and its reasoning tokens counted separately from its output tokens. The judge prompt and rubric are placed as a stable prefix so input caching can apply, which is a determinism and cost-reporting property of the call rather than an optimisation of it.
- Includes: **the calibration judge, GPT-5.6 Luna, over a 10% subsample of judged items.** It exists to test whether the pair carries a shared bias, the question raised by two judges of common geographic origin scoring FR and DE items. Its result is published as its own agreement figure against the pair and is **never folded into any suite's score** — no headline moves because of it, and no row's judged score is derived from it.
- Includes: **the judge protocol as one reusable module**, shipped: versioned judge prompt templates with one variant per item language (EN, FR, DE), versioned rubrics, and a judge call that runs through any provider client behind one interface. Unchanged by this re-targeting, which is the point of the seam.
- Includes: **per-row judge fields, additive** to the existing quality row — each judge's dated model id, the judge prompt id and its content hash, the rubric version, each judge's score, and either the named agreement statistic or the single-judge flag. A judged score carrying neither is not a shape the harness can produce.
- Includes: **independence enforced by model family as a refusal**, not a convention. A judge from the subject's own family is refused with the collision named; it is never silently skipped or quietly substituted. Shipped and provider-agnostic; what changes is which families the judges declare.
- Includes: **the named statistic for an ordinal rubric**, decided here. A 1-5 rubric is treated as the PRD's numeric branch, so the statistic is quadratic-weighted Cohen's kappa with raw agreement beside it; a categorical rubric keeps unweighted kappa (criterion 10). Provider-agnostic and shipped.
- Includes: **the contested rule**. Per-item disagreement above 1 point on the 1-5 ordinal rubric, or a category mismatch on a categorical rubric, publishes the item contested. A contested item stays in the published table with both judges' scores visible; only the headline score excludes it. The threshold is configured per suite and this epic sets that default. Provider-agnostic and shipped.
- Includes: **rate-limit survival on the paid tiers.** The retry, pacing and resume machinery is shipped (`retry.py`, order 5) and applies unchanged to the new pair: retry with backoff honouring the provider's hint, a run that persists every row already produced and marks itself partial naming the failing provider and item, and a resume that never re-issues a judge call whose result is already recorded. The budget is small, so a re-paid call is a real cost rather than a theoretical one.
- Includes: **egress recorded on the calls this epic introduces**. A judged row records that the item and the subject's output left the machine and to which providers, and those provider names change with the pair. Judging is the project's largest egress surface — one judged item is one generation, two judge calls, and a calibration call on one item in ten.
- Includes: a **judged probe of about 10 open-ended items spanning EN, FR and DE**, owned by this epic, written to `aidd_docs/results/judge-probe-reference.jsonl` and never into `quality.jsonl`. It exists to drive the machinery end to end, not to publish a benchmark score.
- Excludes: **the rewriting suite's items and its rubric text.** Story 2 of `quality-scored-comparison-first-three-use-cases` owns them and is this machinery's first real consumer. The probe is not that suite, publishes no score, and does not pre-empt the rubric that suite will define.
- Excludes: criteria 17 and 18, web-research archival and suite shape, including anything specific to judging against a retrieved source set — `aidd_docs/backlog/epics/no-use-case-is-silently-absent.md`.
- Excludes: **showing any of this to a human.** The pitch epic renders judge ids, agreement and the flag, and enforces the refusal to render a bare judged score. This epic makes those fields exist and be correct.
- Excludes: the rest of the row schema and every other methodology criterion. Judge fields are added to the row `every-published-row-explains-and-reproduces-itself` owns, additively, as that epic's own boundaries anticipate.
- Excludes: **the roster's composition and the `family` attribute itself.** `every-size-class-spans-two-families-or-says-it-does-not` supplies `family` and grows `KNOWN_FAMILIES`; this epic consumes them and declares the families its judges belong to. That the judge families in `KNOWN_FAMILIES` must change with the pair is an implied code change recorded here and owned by a story, not decided as an epic boundary.
- Excludes: optimising what judging costs. Cost is reported, never optimised (PRD Non-Goals). Backoff, resumability and a cached stable prefix exist so a rate limit does not destroy a run and so what a call cost is reportable, not so a run gets cheaper.

## Success Evidence

Run the probe against the new pair: about ten open-ended items in three languages, local SLM outputs judged by both GLM and DeepSeek, one item in ten also scored by the calibration judge, and read the resulting rows back. Every judged score names its two judges, the provider that actually answered each call, the prompt that produced it, the rubric it applied, and how far the judges were apart.

Five checks, each able to fail:

- A judged row carrying neither an agreement figure nor the single-judge flag cannot be produced by the harness at all — verified by trying to write one, not by reading the writer.
- Pointing a judge at output from its own model family is refused with the collision named. **No subject the roster now holds triggers this**: the local families (`qwen`, `gemma`, `granite`, `ministral`, `lfm2`, `phi`) and the cloud subjects (`mistral`, `google`) are all independent of `glm` and `deepseek`, so the single-judge path has no live trigger and the probe no longer claims to exercise it with a real row. The refusal is verified by forcing a collision — declaring a subject into a judge's family and watching the call be refused before any backend is invoked — and this epic states plainly that every judged row on the current roster is a two-judge row. The flag stays a shipped invariant against a future roster that does collide, not dead machinery: the writer still refuses a row carrying neither.
- A judged FR item yields two scores, their delta, and a judge prompt each side can be shown to have received in French — read off the row, not off the template source. The calibration judge's score on the FR and DE subsample is published as its own agreement figure beside the pair's, and no suite score moves because of it.
- A row's judge prompt id and content hash re-render the exact prompt that was sent; editing a template changes the hash, and rows written before the edit stay attributable to the text they actually used.
- A run killed mid-probe by a rate limit persists what it produced, reports partial with the failing provider and item named, and resumes without re-issuing the judge calls already paid for — verified by forcing a 429 on a paid tier, not by unit-testing the backoff.

Once `done`, record here what the first real two-judge agreement figure was, whether the 1-point contested threshold survived contact with genuine disagreement, what the calibration judge's agreement with the pair was and whether it differed between EN and the FR/DE items, and what a full judged campaign actually cost against the ten-dollar estimate.
