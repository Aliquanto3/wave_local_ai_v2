# Epic: A score is published with its interval, a difference with its test

Given any published quality score, a reviewer reads the confidence interval around it and the number of licensed, provenance-marked items it was computed over — at least 100 where the suite is built to the publication level — and any claim that two models, engines or variants differ arrives as a paired test over the same items, with its p-value, its direction and its effect size, or it is not published as a difference.

## Context and Value

The audience is the one the PRD adds for the publication: "As an academic or technical reviewer, I want the sample size, the confidence interval and the significance test behind every published claim, so that I can tell a real difference between two models from noise in a small suite." Two questions come before every other one a reviewer asks — how many items, and is the difference distinguishable from noise — and the repository currently answers neither.

The publication asks three questions: which model, which engine or prompt variant, and which machine class. This epic's outputs reach the first two, because a paired test compares two configurations on identical items whatever dimension separates them, so a model-versus-model and a variant-versus-variant claim each arrive with its test. They do not reach the third. A machine-class answer is assembled from a refusal record, a throughput median with its spread flag, and a quality score the machine epic assumes machine-invariant, and none of the three is produced here — by the same reasoning that excludes runtime from inferential treatment below. Stated so the backlog does not read a machine-class answer into this epic.

Verified current state, at `0f849c8`:

- `suite_gate.py` knows one level. `MIN_SUITE_ITEMS = 20`, `MIN_LANGUAGE_SHARE = 0.25`, `MIN_PER_LANGUAGE_CELL_ITEMS = 10`, and `gate_suite` returns `indicative` with its reasons. There is no publication level, and no row field naming which level a score was produced at.
- `_VALID_PROVENANCE` already accepts `licensed`, but nothing records *which* licence, and neither an item nor a suite records a source benchmark or the rule by which a subset was selected. `contamination_risk` is already forced to `provenance == "public"`: the marking the publication suites need exists, and the items it would mark do not.
- `scoring.py` publishes `SuiteScore`, `GradedSuiteScore` and per-language cells. There is no interval anywhere in the codebase. `agreement.py` is the only module that looks inferential, and it measures two judges against each other — an agreement statistic is not an uncertainty, and neither becomes the other.
- The published arithmetic (`aidd_docs/results/README.md`): `classification-support-routing` holds 20 items, `en` n=10, `fr` n=5, `de` n=5. One item moves the suite score 5 points and a per-language cell 20. `translation-business-short-form` holds 21 items in three directions, seven per direction, and all three of its cells publish as indicative. Two models 10 accuracy points apart are two items apart. Nothing published today distinguishes that from noise, and nothing published says so beyond the word `indicative`.

The PRD already promises the whole of it, in three places, and the code implements none of it: Methodology 4 (a declared suite level, 100 items at publication level, subsets of named public benchmarks with each benchmark's licence and the selection rule recorded), Methodology 24 (a bootstrap confidence interval with its resample count, a paired Wilcoxon with p-value, direction and paired n), and acceptance criteria 131 and 135, which state both as things a published run must show. This epic is the distance between that promise and the repository.

Why both suite levels survive rather than one replacing the other: a publication-level suite drawn from public benchmarks is contamination-marked almost item for item, because a public model may have trained on it. The hand-written 20-item suites are the clean but small counterweight, uncontaminated and CC-BY 4.0 owned by the repo, and their rows are already published. A large contaminated score and a small clean one are different claims, and neither answers the reviewer alone. So the publication suites arrive as new suite ids beside the existing ones, and a published table names the level of every row rather than averaging across them.

One property of the row shape makes the paired half cheaper than it looks: a quality row is written per item and already carries `item_id` and `item_score`/`correct` beside `suite_score`/`suite_accuracy` (`row_contract.REQUIRED_FIELDS["quality"]`). Per-item scores are therefore already published, so a paired test between two configurations is a join on `item_id` over the bundle. Nothing new has to be stored per item, and a third party can recompute the test from the published bundle alone — which is the reason for publishing it at all.

## Boundaries

- Includes: **a second level in the suite gate**. `publication` requires at least 100 items, keeps the 25% per-language share, and requires every item to carry a licence and a source; `development` stays at 20 and is unchanged. `gate_suite` returns the level it certified and every row names it, so a suite that declares `publication` and holds 80 items fails to the level it declared rather than passing quietly at the other one.
- Includes: **licence and source on the item, selection rule on the suite.** Per item: the licence under which that item is redistributed — the source's own for a drawn item, CC-BY 4.0 for the repo's hand-written ones — and the source it was drawn from with that source's revision. Per suite: each benchmark it draws from, that benchmark's licence, and the selection rule as data: a recorded seed, a sampler version and the source revision, so re-running the selection returns the same item ids and the answer to "how did you pick these hundred" is a command rather than a sentence. Three properties make that claim hold outside the machine that wrote it. Each drawn item carries a content hash over its own normalised text, so an upstream retag or a silently edited item is nameable at the item rather than only at the set's `prompt_set_hash`, and a fetched item can be proven to be the item that was scored. The selection rule states the canonicalisation applied before sampling — source rows sorted by a named stable source key, that key named on the suite, with the loader library and its version recorded beside the sampler version — because a seeded draw is a function of the seed and of the order the source arrived in. And the sampler is stratified by construction, by language and, for a classification source, by label, so the first seed satisfies the gate by design rather than by luck; where a seed is nonetheless retried, the attempt count and every seed tried are recorded, since an unrecorded retry loop turns a reproducibility claim into selection on the outcome.
- Includes: **two publication-level suites, standing beside the two that exist** — one classification, one translation, each at 100 items or more, each of EN, FR and DE at 25% or more, drawn as seeded subsets of named public benchmarks. They take new suite ids. `classification-support-routing` and `translation-business-short-form` keep their identity, their items, their CC-BY 4.0 licence, their published rows and their `development` level.
- Includes: **bootstrap confidence intervals over the items of a scored batch**, on the suite score and on each per-language cell, for the exact-match and the graded scorer alike, at both levels. Four values are fixed here rather than deferred to a story plan, because each of them is a published number and two implementations free to choose differently publish different intervals from the same rows: a 95% confidence level, 10 000 resamples, resampling stratified within language for a per-language cell and unstratified for the suite score, and a named draw procedure — generator identity and version, draw order, tie handling and percentile interpolation rule — published as a versioned algorithm id. The interval method itself is the one value left open, in Dependencies, with a recommendation. The row carries the interval, the confidence level, the resample count, the seed, the method and the draw-procedure id beside the score they qualify, the same way `language_breakdown` already rides every row of a batch.
- Includes: **a minimum detectable effect published beside every interval**, per suite and per scoring kind: the smallest difference the suite could have resolved at the published confidence level, read off the same resample rather than computed a second way. It is the cheapest output in the epic and the one that most changes how the table reads, because without it "not distinguishable" reads as "the same" — the misreading this epic exists to prevent. On a binary-scored 20-item suite nothing under roughly 20 points is resolvable; at 100 items, roughly 10.
- Includes: **a named null reason wherever a statistic is undefined or misleading**, on the shape `agreement.py` already uses for Cohen's kappa: a value and exactly one non-null reason, never both. A suite scored 1.0 or 0.0, whose every percentile resample returns the same value and whose interval collapses to zero width; McNemar with no discordant pairs; a discordant-pair odds ratio with an empty cell; and a per-language cell at n=0, where `score_suite_by_language` already returns `accuracy=0.0, n=0` — each is a named reason rather than a certainty, an undefined p or an infinity published as a number. A zero-width interval on a perfect 20-item score is the most quotable wrong number this epic could ship.
- Includes: **a paired test between two published configurations on identical items**, named per scoring kind, reported with p-value, direction, effect size, the number of paired items, the number that could not be paired and which side each unpaired item was missing from. Direction is expressed against a declared `reference_run_id` and `candidate_run_id`, with every difference computed as candidate minus reference, so a published direction cannot flip on an implementation detail; `verdict.py` already carries `reference_run_id` and the name is reused rather than reinvented. The test names its `compared_field`, the same name `verdict.py` already uses, and carries the tie count and the zero-difference count beside the paired n, because a p-value over 100 items does not say how many of those pairs carried information. The signed-rank test's zero-difference convention, its exact-versus-normal-approximation rule and that rule's n threshold, its continuity correction, and the rank-biserial formula by its definition are all named on the record: Methodology 9 keeps a failed generation in the denominator as a zero, so an item both sides failed is an exact tie, and dropping those pairs rather than keeping them is a different published p.
- Includes: **a tracked comparison record** as its own artifact in the reference bundle — the two run ids, the suite id and version both sides ran, the paired item ids, the compared field, the test that was run, its statistic, p, direction, effect size, n, the unpairable counts, and the set of row fields on which the two sides actually differ, computed from the rows rather than declared. It is produced by a named analysis command over the bundle, not at read time by a view, and it refuses rather than reports when the two sides are not comparable. It carries one derived, reader-facing verdict — distinguishable, not distinguishable, or not comparable, evaluated against the declared family at the declared alpha — with every raw input kept beside it: the pitch epic can only render a field this epic creates, and neither of the PRD's two non-academic readers of published numbers reads a rank-biserial correlation.
- Includes: **the comparison family as the unit of the artifact, not an attribute of a member.** Holm is a function of a whole family's p-values, so an adjusted p written on a record when the family held ten comparisons is arithmetically wrong the moment an eleventh runs. One analysis invocation writes one family record holding every comparison it ran, the family's definition, its size, and each member's adjusted p computed over that closed set. A family is immutable once written; adding a comparison writes a new family record superseding the old by id, which is the row epic's supersede-don't-backfill discipline rather than a violation of it. The default family is one suite by one dimension, closed at analysis time, and a family of one states an adjusted p equal to its raw p rather than omitting the field.
- Includes: **the tabular export carrying the interval and the comparison record**, so the third-party researcher of the PRD's user story re-analyses the published results without cloning and running the project.
- Includes: **at least one published batch per new suite in the reference bundle**, under the row epic's established supersede-don't-backfill discipline: new rows are new rows, and no already-published row is retro-fitted with an interval it was never written with.
- Includes: **the threshold review the PRD promises.** Once a publication suite has run, a per-language cell holds 25 items or more and the 10-item indicative floor can no longer fire on its own arithmetic. The review states whether that floor still means anything, whether 20 remains the right development floor, and whether the intervals observed at 100 items make 100 look sufficient or thin — and amends Methodology 4, or leaves it, in writing either way. One thing it must say rather than imply: at the 25% minimum share a per-language cell at 100 items holds about 25 items and resolves nothing under roughly a 25-point gap, so the floor ceasing to fire is not the cell becoming readable, and removing an `indicative` mark is itself a claim. The review states whether the publication size target belongs against the suite or against the language cell.
- Includes: **declaring this epic's new contract fields unrendered in the view partition.** `read_model.py` partitions each view's field set against `row_contract` rather than maintaining it by hand, so every field added here — the level, the item licence, the item source and its revision, the interval and its five qualifiers — fails the build until it is either rendered or deliberately declared unrendered. This epic declares them unrendered. Whether the pitch renders any of them is that epic's call and is not decided here.
- Excludes: **new use cases and new use-case suites.** `no-use-case-is-silently-absent` owns which suites exist. This epic adds a second suite to two use cases that are already exercised; the coverage record's `exercised` entry for classification and translation gains a second suite id, a field addition agreed there, never a new entry here.
- Excludes: **the suite definition shape and the registry that resolves a suite id to a definition** — the same epic. Two suites for one use case is precisely what the registry exists for; this epic consumes it and adds three fields to the shape it defines.
- Excludes: **the size, language and provenance gate itself, the row contract, the writer gate and the reproduction verdict** — `every-published-row-explains-and-reproduces-itself` owns all four. This epic adds one level to that gate and four fields to that contract, and rebuilds neither. The reproduction verdict stays a re-run verdict over one row; it does not become a statistical test, and a confidence interval never decides it.
- Excludes: **machines, compute modes, inference engines, prompt variants and agentic harnesses.** Each is a further row dimension owned elsewhere. A paired test compares two configurations on identical items whatever dimension separates them, so nothing here needs those dimensions to exist first and nothing here changes when they arrive.
- Excludes: **the judge machinery, judge independence, the agreement statistic and any rubric.** The interval and the test are scoring-kind agnostic and apply to a judged score once judged rows exist; `agreement.py` stays what it is.
- Excludes: **the runtime median-and-spread treatment of Methodology 6 and 7**, deliberately and by the PRD's own reasoning. Runtime is a repeated measurement of one machine, not a sample drawn from a population, and an interval or a significance test on it would claim more than the design supports. No runtime field changes in this epic.
- Excludes: **roster composition** — which models and families exist is its own epic — and **rendering any of this to a decision-maker.** `the-pitch-runs-from-a-browser-and-only-with-the-key` owns the views; this epic makes the numbers exist and be correct, not readable at a glance.
- Excludes: **a winner committed before the evidence** — the PRD's actual non-goal, which forbids "selecting one 'winning' web-search tool or dense model **up front**", not a ranking derived from the published tests. A test says whether a difference is distinguishable from noise; it never says whether it matters. Whether a derived leader set — the models not distinguishable from the best, per suite and machine class — is published at all, and by which epic, is open and recorded in Dependencies rather than settled here.

### Decisions this epic takes

Nine of these widen a criterion beyond the PRD's wording and are marked `→ PRD`; the PRD is where they are settled once the epic proves them.

| Subject | Decision taken here |
| --- | --- |
| The two levels coexist | A publication-level suite is a new suite id beside the hand-written one, not a new version of it and not a replacement. The hand-written items are the only uncontaminated evidence the project owns and their rows are already published; a public subset is contamination-marked item for item. A published table names the level of every row and never averages across the two. |
| A seed alone does not reproduce an interval | An unseeded resample returns a different interval on every recomputation, which makes the row unreproducible under criterion 1 and hands the re-run verdict a difference nobody introduced. A seed on its own is no better: `random.Random(7)`, `numpy.random.default_rng(7)` and a library's own bootstrap draw three different resample sequences from it, so two implementations publish different intervals from the same recorded seed and nothing on the row detects it. The row therefore carries six values, not one — the confidence level (95%), the resample count (10 000), the interval method, the seed, the generator identity and version, and the draw procedure as a versioned algorithm id whose published definition covers draw order, tie handling and percentile interpolation. Same reasoning that already puts `metric_id`, `metric_version` and `metric_params` on a graded row so a third party recomputing chrF with sacreBLEU can catch us. → PRD |
| Failed generations resample with everything else | Criterion 9 puts a failed generation in the denominator as a zero. It stays in the resampled item set. An interval computed over the successes alone qualifies a score nobody published. |
| The paired test is named per scoring kind | Wilcoxon signed-rank over per-item differences is right for a graded or ordinal score — chrF, a 1-5 rubric. It is not right for a binary exact-match score: every non-zero difference is ±1, ties are dropped, and what survives is a sign test over the discordant pairs. A binary-scored suite, which classification is today, carries McNemar's exact test on the discordant pairs instead, and the comparison record names which test it ran and why. A Wilcoxon printed over a binary vector is exactly the finding this epic exists to keep out of a reviewer's hands. → PRD |
| A direction is not an effect size | The record carries a matched-pairs effect size beside p and direction: rank-biserial correlation for the signed-rank test, the discordant-pair odds ratio for McNemar's. A p-value over 100 items answers "distinguishable" and never "by how much", and a table of statistically significant half-point differences is the failure mode a larger suite introduces rather than removes. → PRD |
| Multiplicity is stated, and the family is the artifact | A full roster produces many pairwise comparisons, and an unadjusted family of p-values is the reviewer's second question. But a Holm-adjusted p is a function of the whole family, so it cannot be a property of one record: written when the family held ten, it is wrong the moment an eleventh runs, and rewriting the siblings would break the supersede-don't-backfill discipline this epic adopts. One analysis invocation writes one immutable family record — its definition, its size, every comparison it ran, and each member's adjusted p over that closed set — superseded by id when the family grows. The default family is one suite by one dimension; a family of one carries an adjusted p equal to its raw p rather than omitting the field. → PRD |
| A comparison refuses rather than reports, and "item set" means the declared one | A different suite id, a different `suite_version`, a different declared item set (`prompt_set_hash`) or two different levels produce a refusal naming the mismatch, not a p-value over whatever happened to overlap. Silently intersecting two declared item sets publishes a test over a suite that has no name. The *observed* row set is a different thing and is not a refusal: a resumed or partially failed batch produces rows on one side only, and that is the paired n plus the one-sided count below. The refusal list extends beyond the suite to everything Methodology 3 requires identical across compared models — `max_output_tokens`, `stop_sequences`, `context_length`, `thinking_policy` — to the metric triple on a graded row, and to a scoring-kind or `compared_field` mismatch, because a truncation cap that manufactures failures on one side is read by the test as quality. The refusal names the differing field, on `verdict.py`'s existing `differing_fields` convention rather than a second one. → PRD |
| Unpairable items are counted, and named to a side | Both the paired n and the count of items present on one side only sit on the record, with which side each unpaired item was missing from. A resume gap, a machine refusal or a failed batch shrinks a comparison, and the shrinkage is evidence rather than noise to be tidied away; a reviewer's first question about a shrunken comparison is which side shrank. This is not hypothetical — `aidd_docs/results/README.md` records `mistral-small-2603` producing no rows at all in two runs after its retry budget was exhausted. |
| The interval is published at both levels | A development-level score keeps its `indicative` marking and gains an interval as well. A wide interval on 20 items is the disclosure; withholding it below 100 items would hide the exact property that makes a small number readable. → PRD |
| The licence lives on the item, not only on the suite | A suite may draw from more than one source under more than one term, and the bundle is redistributed item by item. A suite-level licence would be a claim about items it does not describe. |
| Provenance and licence stay author declarations | Unchanged from the row epic's own statement of the limit: nothing verifies that a declared licence or a declared source is the true one. Stated so the limit is disclosed rather than discovered. |
| An undefined statistic publishes a named reason, never a number | `agreement.py` already refuses to publish a mathematically defined but misleading `κ = 0`, returning a value and exactly one non-null reason from `zero_variance`, `zero_expected_disagreement`, `insufficient_items`. The interval, the p-value and the effect size take the same shape, with the degenerate cases enumerated as named reasons: a suite scored 1.0 or 0.0 whose interval collapses to zero width, McNemar with no discordant pairs, an odds ratio with an empty cell, a language cell at n=0. A published 0.95 on 20 items is one item from the first of those. → PRD |
| A detection limit is published beside the interval | A minimum detectable effect per suite and scoring kind, read off the same resample. It is the number that stops "not distinguishable" being read as "the same", and it is the difference between an honest small result and a misleading one. → PRD |
| 100 items is the size at which this project's own questions can return an answer | The PRD's bar is reviewer convention; the binding reason is arithmetic the epic's other decisions create. Holm over the prompt-variant epic's four variants is a family of six, rejecting nothing unless the smallest raw p is at most 0.0083. McNemar's exact two-sided p over `n` one-directional discordant pairs is 2·0.5ⁿ, reaching 0.0083 only at `n` = 8. So on a 20-item suite a variant must flip eight of twenty items all one way — a 40% swing — before these rules permit calling it a difference; mixed discordance needs more still, and widening the family to an eight-model roster's 28 comparisons raises the floor to eleven. The floor is absolute rather than proportional, which is exactly why suite size resolves it: eight discordant pairs out of 100 is an ordinary effect. Below 100, a negative result records `n` rather than a finding. |
| The statistics ship in-repo on the standard library, with scipy as a dev-only oracle | A stdlib percentile bootstrap of 10 000 resamples over 100 items runs in 0.18 s, so performance is not an argument. Every statistic named here has a stdlib-exact form: McNemar's exact test is a two-sided binomial over the discordant pairs (`math.comb`), Holm and rank-biserial are a sort and arithmetic, and the closed-form reference check needs `statistics.NormalDist().inv_cdf`. The bundle epic took the same decision one dimension over — CSV emitted with the standard library alone — for a harness whose target machines include one with 16 GB and no GPU, and a new runtime dependency is a permanent addition to the OSV audit surface. `numpy` and `pandas` are installed only transitively via `codecarbon` and are not available to depend on. `scipy` is added as a **dev-only** test oracle: an independent implementation to cross-validate against is stronger than a hand-computed fixture and cheaper to maintain, and where a convention differs from scipy's, the test that shows the difference documents the convention. |
| The licence spike returns one of three verdicts, each with its consequence written in advance | Permissive, share-alike, or no-redistribution — not a binary. The manifest fallback is the remedy for none of them as the epic first wrote it, because a published quality row already redistributes the item verbatim: `REQUIRED_FIELDS["quality"]` requires `prompt` and `expected_label`, and `GRADED_FIELDS` requires `reference_output`. Withholding the suite definition while shipping the rows costs the bundle its self-sufficiency and still redistributes the content. So: permissive ships items and rows unchanged; no-redistribution requires the rows themselves to carry `prompt`, `expected_label` and `reference_output` redacted to the per-item content hash, which makes the score unrecomputable from the bundle and is a far larger cost than a manifest; share-alike requires segregating those items and their derived rows under their own licence file, since the bundle is fixed at CC-BY 4.0 and an SA source cannot be absorbed into it. The spike's exit criterion is evaluated against the row contract, not only against `suite-definitions/`, and the share-alike question is checked first because it is the branch the named leads are likeliest to take. → PRD |

## Success Evidence

Thirteen checks, each able to fail. The first eleven are code-level and run on constructed suites, rows and fixtures; the last two need a real run and the licence answer.

Nine of the first eleven are also demonstrable on rows already in the bundle, at zero compute and zero API cost. `aidd_docs/results/quality-reference.jsonl` already holds two complete pairable comparisons — same `suite_id`, same `suite_version`, the same 20 item ids, two models — because a quality row is already per item. Run over them, run `5e13166d` pairs Qwen3.6-35B-A3B at 0.80 against mistral-small-2603 at 0.95 with 4/1 discordant pairs, McNemar exact p = 0.375; run `d20afbda` pairs the same models at 0.80 against 0.90 with 4/2 discordant, p = 0.688. A 15-point accuracy gap that a reviewer cannot distinguish from noise, on numbers this repository has already published, is the epic's entire thesis. Nothing in the inferential half waits on a publication suite or on the licence answer.

- A suite declaring `publication` with 99 items fails the gate to that level naming the count; the same suite at 100 with one language below 25% fails naming the language; `classification-support-routing` still passes at `development` unchanged, and rows from both name the level they were produced at.
- The subset selection, re-run from its recorded seed, sampler version and source revision, returns the same item ids in the same order. Changing the seed changes them; changing nothing changes nothing.
- The bootstrap reproduces a hand-computed interval on a small fixture, and on a proportion agrees with a **Wilson** 95% reference interval — one reference, named in advance, because an implementation free to pick whichever of Clopper-Pearson and Wilson it lands nearer is validated against nothing — to within **0.01 on each bound**, at n=20 and p=0.80 and at n=100 and p=0.80. A stdlib percentile bootstrap over the published classification rows gives [0.570, 0.750] against Wilson's [0.563, 0.745], so the tolerance is met with room and is a real bar rather than a formality.
- The recorded interval block, replayed — seed, generator identity and version, draw procedure id, resample count, method, confidence level — returns the identical interval bit for bit. Today's fixture check passes for two implementations that publish different numbers and discriminates neither; this one does not.
- Each named null reason is produced by the state that names it and by no other: a suite scored 1.0 returns the zero-width reason rather than `[1.0, 1.0]`, McNemar with no discordant pairs returns its reason rather than a p, an odds ratio with an empty cell returns its reason rather than an infinity, and a language cell at n=0 returns its reason rather than an interval around 0.0.
- Three invariants hold on every published batch: the point estimate lies inside its own interval; the n the interval was computed over equals the n already published in `language_breakdown` and `score_breakdown`; and every row of one batch carries the identical interval block, which is what makes the interval a batch statistic rather than a per-row one. They fail loudly when the bootstrap and the score were computed over different item sets, which a resumed batch is the ordinary way to produce.
- The two paired tests reproduce a published worked example each, a signed-rank table and a McNemar 2x2, and the classification suite routes to McNemar's rather than to Wilcoxon without anyone choosing per call site.
- Two published rows from the same suite id and version produce a record carrying paired n, the compared field, the test named, raw and adjusted p, direction against the declared reference run, effect size, the tie and zero-difference counts, the unpairable counts named to a side, the computed set of differing row fields, and one reader-facing verdict. The same two rows with one side's `suite_version` bumped produce a refusal naming the version rather than a number; so does a differing `max_output_tokens`, a differing `thinking_policy`, a differing metric version on a graded row, and a scoring-kind mismatch — each naming its own field.
- A comparison whose two sides differ on more than one row field is published as an observation naming the confound, or refused, and never as a clean paired test. A `gpu` row against a `cpu_only` row of the same model produces a well-formed record whose differing-field set says so, so a significant p there reads as the bug signal it is.
- A family record's Holm-adjusted p-values are computed over the closed set it holds; adding a twelfth comparison to a family of eleven writes a new family record superseding the old by id, and no already-published record is rewritten. A family of one carries an adjusted p equal to its raw p.
- Recomputing every interval and every comparison from the published bundle alone, through the same analysis command and no private path, returns identical values — the third-party re-analysis of the PRD's user story, run against ourselves first.
- Both publication suites exist at 100 items or more, with EN, FR and DE each at 25% or more, every item carrying a licence and a source, and one published batch per suite sits in the reference bundle with intervals on its rows.
- The threshold review is written, and it is checkable rather than merely produced. It cites the observed interval widths at n=100 against those at n=20 for the same use case, the observed per-language cell counts, the number of times the 10-item floor fired on the publication run, and the observed minimum detectable effect at both sizes; it is wrong if any figure it cites disagrees with the published rows. On that evidence it states whether the 10-item per-language floor ever fires again once cells hold 25 items, whether 20 remains the right development floor, whether 100 looks sufficient or thin, and whether the size target belongs against the suite or against the language cell — recorded as an amendment to Methodology 4 or as an explicit decision to leave it, never as silence.

Falsification the epic accepts in advance: if the licence spike finds no candidate source whose terms permit redistributing a subset inside the bundle's CC-BY 4.0, the outcome as written is not reachable. The fallback is the three-rung ladder in the Decisions table, not a manifest — a manifest withholds the suite definition while the rows keep redistributing `prompt`, `expected_label` and `reference_output` verbatim, so it protects nothing. On the no-redistribution rung the rows themselves carry those three fields redacted to the per-item content hash, which costs the bundle not only the self-sufficiency `aidd_docs/results/README.md` states as its whole property but the ability to recompute the score from what is published; on the share-alike rung the drawn items and their derived rows are segregated under their own licence file rather than absorbed into a CC-BY 4.0 bundle. Whichever rung, the trade is written into that README rather than absorbed in silence.

Once `done`, record here what the intervals actually were at 100 items against the ones the 20-item suites imply, how many of the paired comparisons run on real rows survived adjustment, and whether the publication and development levels ranked the roster the same way — a disagreement between them is a contamination signal and is worth more than either score alone. That last observation is read off the two published tables by hand and is deliberately not a computed artifact: the two levels are different suite ids over different items, so the comparison record refuses them by design, and no rank-agreement statistic is in scope here.
